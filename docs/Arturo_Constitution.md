# The Constitution of Arturo

Everything else should derive from these principles.

## Plain-language promise

Arturo should make five things easy to see:

1. who owns an ability;
2. what work happened;
3. where information came from;
4. which rules controlled the work; and
5. which decisions belonged to Humans.

The detailed principles below explain how.

## I. Enterprises are collections of Business Functions

Departments, people, agents, models, and tools are not the primary unit. A Business Function is an enduring organisational capability with explicit purpose and ownership.

## II. Every Business Function owns an outcome

Responsibility must be visible. Work should not become orphaned merely because a Worker, workflow, or technology changes.

## III. Workers execute; Business Functions persist

AI Workers, Human Workers, models, suppliers, and tools are replaceable execution resources. Registration describes participation; it does not prove identity, expertise, employment, or authority.

## IV. Runtimes coordinate enduring operation

A Business Function Runtime evaluates signals, creates warranted Jobs, routes work, observes outcomes, and reports operational state. One-off workflows are temporary and do not replace the enduring Function.

## V. Jobs have traceable lifecycles

A Job may be queued, governed, assigned, started, completed, failed, or rejected. Its current state can change. Durable Events and Timeline records preserve what happened and why; corrections are additive rather than silent rewrites of history.

## VI. Evidence carries provenance

Model synthesis, human assertions, external records, and verified evidence are not interchangeable. Arturo must expose origin, uncertainty, and execution eligibility instead of turning plausible text into organisational truth.

## VII. Memory is curated, scoped, and correctable

Operational Memory exists to support future work, not to preserve every generated conclusion. Sensitive, irrelevant, unverified, or superseded content must be excluded, quarantined, scoped, or removed according to policy.

## VIII. Health is measured, not imagined

Business Functions should be assessed through explicit operational indicators such as outcomes, failures, backlog, latency, cost, quality, governed criteria, and risk. Health is evidence about operation, not a model's declaration of business success.

## IX. Governance precedes consequential execution

Deterministic policy defines allowed, denied, and approval-gated boundaries. Model reasoning may inform a proposal but cannot silently override policy, authority, or trust boundaries.

## X. Humans remain accountable where authority requires them

Human participation must be attributable and reviewable. Approval authorises an action; it does not certify the eventual output. Arturo should never impersonate human judgment or professional authority.

## XI. External action is explicit

Generating text is not sending an email, changing a database, deploying software, publishing content, or controlling equipment. External action requires a configured capability, validated input, least privilege, policy evaluation, and auditable confirmation.

## XII. Improvement must remain governed

The Enterprise may learn and evolve only from eligible evidence through controlled change. Self-improvement must not become unreviewed self-modification.

## XIII. Every abstraction must make the organisation simpler

Arturo should make ownership, work, evidence, authority, and change easier to understand as automation increases. Complexity that cannot be inspected or governed does not belong in the operating model.
