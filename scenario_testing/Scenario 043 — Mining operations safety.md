Background
Ironvale Mining operates under the principle of operating safely with evidence-led analysis and accountable human control of every operational action. The geotechnical analysis function aims to provide synthetic scenarios for review, enabling informed decision-making.

Scenario Overview
Slope-monitoring data suggests movement near an active bench in a mining operation. This incident requires immediate attention to prevent potential hazards.

Exclusion-Zone Considerations

To assess the situation, it is essential to identify the exclusion zone around the active bench. The exclusion zone should be determined based on factors such as:

–  Distance from the active bench
–  Geotechnical characteristics (e.g., rock type, stability)
–  Mining activities in progress
–  Potential impact zones for equipment or personnel

High-Level Stabilisation Options

The following options can be considered to stabilize the area:

1. Drilling and blasting: Inserting drill bits and explosives to stabilize the slope.
2. Piling and grouting: Installing piles and grouting them in place to support the slope.
3. Scaffolding and bracing: Constructing scaffolding and bracing systems to provide temporary support.

Decisions for Mine Manager

The mine manager must make decisions based on the following factors:

–  Severity of movement: Assess the extent of the movement and its potential impact on the mining operation.
–  Geotechnical risks: Evaluate the stability of the surrounding rock and potential hazards.
–  Equipment and personnel safety: Consider the risk to equipment and personnel in the vicinity.
–  Mining operational requirements: Balance stabilisation efforts with ongoing mining activities.

Next Steps

To proceed, the mine manager should

1. Conduct a site assessment to determine the exclusion zone.
2. Evaluate the stability of the surrounding rock using geotechnical parameters.
3. Consult with experts (e.g., geologists, engineers) for guidance on high-level stabilisation options.
4. Make informed decisions based on the analysis and consult with relevant stakeholders.

**Note**                          This analysis is a synthetic scenario and should not be taken as operational instruction. The mine manager must exercise their judgment and expertise to make decisions suitable for the specific situation.
