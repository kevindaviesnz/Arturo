Scenario Overview

A recent increase in trap-line data indicates a growing presence of predators (e.g., stoats, ferrets) in the vicinity of a sensitive nesting site. The nesting site is home to a protected species, and a rapid increase in predator numbers poses a significant threat to its survival.

High-Level Response Options

To address this scenario, the following high-level response options are considered:

1. Cordon off the area: Temporarily restrict access to the nesting site and surrounding area to prevent further predation.
2. Hunter-assisted control: Deploy trained hunters to humanely remove predators from the area.
3. Trapping program: Implement a trapping program to capture and relocate or euthanize predators.

Non-Target Risks

The proposed response options carry non-target risks, including:

–  Unintended harm to other species, such as native birds or mammals
–  Negative impacts on local ecosystems and biodiversity
–  Potential effects on human safety and well-being

Field Decisions for the Ranger

To mitigate these risks, the ranger must consider the following factors when making field decisions:

1. Species identification: Ensure accurate identification of the predator species to inform control measures.
2. Nesting site conditions: Assess the condition and vulnerability of the nesting site to predation.
3. Predator population density: Evaluate the current predator population density to determine the effectiveness of control measures.
4. Local regulations and policies: Consider relevant local laws, regulations, and conservation policies.

The ranger must weigh these factors against the need to protect the sensitive species and ensure a safe and effective response.

Recommendations

Further information is required to inform this analysis. The following data are needed:
