4. Time constraints for each vessel's arrival

Sequencing Options

At a high level, the operations manager must consider the following sequencing options:

1. Alternate Tide Windows: Schedule one vessel in the early tide window and the other in the late tide window.
2. Parallel Berthing: Allow both vessels to berth simultaneously, if possible.
3. Queueing: Place vessel B on a waiting list for the available berthing slot.

Knock-on Yard Effects

Each sequencing option has potential knock-on effects:

1. Alternate Tide Windows:
–  May lead to increased labor costs due to separate handling and storage procedures.
–  Could result in reduced yard capacity utilization.
2. Parallel Berthing:
–  Requires more advanced planning and coordination to ensure safe and efficient berthing.
–  May increase the risk of vessel collisions or damage.
3. Queueing:
–  May lead to delays for both vessels, impacting their schedules and cargo delivery deadlines.
–  Could result in additional storage costs for vessel B.

Operations Manager Decisions

The operations manager must weigh these factors against the constraints of:

1. Time-sensitive cargo deliveries
2. Yard resource availability
3. Berth capacity limitations

Ultimately, the operations manager will need to make a decision based on the specific circumstances and priorities of each vessel.
